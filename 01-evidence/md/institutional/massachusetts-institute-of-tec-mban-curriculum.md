# MBAn Curriculum


The 12-month Master of Business Analytics (MBAn) program at the MIT Sloan School of Management


combines advanced coursework in machine learning, optimization, and analytics with hands-on experience


solving real organizational challenges. Students build technical expertise alongside the communication and


[management skills needed to turn data into informed decisions. They can also choose from approved](https://orc.mit.edu/academics/course-offerings/)


[analytics electives from the MIT Operations Research Center (ORC) to tailor part of the curriculum to their](https://orc.mit.edu/academics/course-offerings/)


interests and goals.


[Course titles, numbers, instructors, and descriptions are subject to change. Consult the MIT subject listing](http://student.mit.edu/catalog/m15a.html)


for the most current information.




## How Is the 12-Month MBAn Curriculum Structured?

Master of Business Analytics students complete 111 to 147 units of coursework across the fall, Independent


Activities Period (IAP), spring, and summer terms. Required courses build technical and management


expertise, while approved analytics and unrestricted electives allow students to tailor parts of the


curriculum.

## How Does the MIT Sloan Analytics Capstone Work?


[The MBAn curriculum includes an Analytics Capstone in which student teams work with sponsor](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/programs/master-business-analytics/mit-sloan-analytics-capstone)


organizations in the United States and abroad to solve real-world data science challenges. From February


through May, students work on campus under the guidance of faculty advisors before joining their host


organizations full time during the summer.


The Capstone provides a tuition offset of more than $22,000. After completing the experience and


submitting a final report and presentation in August, students graduate with their degree.

## Research Opportunities Available to MBAn Students


MBAn students may apply for research assistantships with affiliated faculty and senior staff from the MIT


Sloan School of Management and the MIT Operations Research Center. These opportunities allow students


to explore a range of applications and methods in operations research and analytics.
