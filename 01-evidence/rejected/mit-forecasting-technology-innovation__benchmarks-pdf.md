**Why Is Forecasting**
**Technology Innovation**
**Important**


Decision makers who are skilled at forecasting technology innovation can have a
competitive advantage.


**Investors can stay ahead of industry trends and build a successful technology**
**investment portfolio.**


**Scientists and engineers can better prioritize resources invested in technology research**
**and development.**


**Companies can be more successful at anticipating and entering new and promising**

**markets.**


**Governments can support national competitiveness and advance technology for the**
**benefit of society.**


The better these decision makers are at measuring and forecasting technology innovation,
the more effective their decision-making processes around technology investment can be.

**Industry Applications**


Any organization looking to gain a competitive advantage by improving existing technologies
and implementing new ones can benefit from an advanced, data-informed decision-making
process. This course will be particularly relevant to:



Technology innovation is essential to the evolving
healthcare industry. New technologies involving AI, data
science, and analytics are poised to improve various facets
of healthcare. For example, to eliminate future supply
chain disruptions 50% of life science companies and
healthcare providers are set to boost investments in AI and
advanced analytics (Limaye, 2021). However, questions
remain about how to successfully implement these
new technologies within existing processes, and which
technologies to prioritize. Successful technology innovation
strategies promise to further understanding of the genetic
factors behind disease, optimize clinical studies and trials,
and streamline regulatory approval processes.


86% of manufacturers in the US have stated that smart

factories and their associated technologies would be the most
important drivers of competition by 2025 (Laaper et al., 2020).
The extensive list of potential supporting technologies for this
transition— including IoT, data analytics, cloud computing, and
machine learning and AI, among others—highlights the need for
a data-driven, selective approach to technology investment and
design in a sector marked by heightened competition. If equipped
with the expertise and tools necessary to predict and influence
the evolution of these technologies, organizations stand to gain a
substantial advantage over their competitors.

Investing in technology innovation means working with
uncertainty. While no crystal ball exists, data-driven forecasting
models and mechanistic models of innovation can point to fast
improving industries and particularly promising technologies
within them. This information is essential for balancing portfolios

of investments to achieve success. Whether investments are

being made in early-stage technologies or more advanced
ones, it is important to recognize both trends and noise in
the development of technologies to appropriately diversify or
concentrate investments. Using data-informed methods to
measure and forecast technological change, investment firms
can gain a significant advantage


The global energy market is experiencing a rapid transition
driven by fundamental changes in technological development,
regulations, consumer preferences, and investor sentiments.
The evergrowing pressure to reduce greenhouse gas emissions
through domestic and international policy and through
corporate sustainability programs is having a significant impact
on the choices of technologies that serve the energy sector.
Simultaneously, the shifting of energy portfolios through increased
electrification, emerging alternate fuel and energy storage options,
and efficiency gains are further advancing the need for new and
innovative technology solutions. The challenge is to be armed with
the best tools to understand and act on the most effective energy
options to meet short and long-term market demands.
(McKinsey, 2022).

The chemical industry is directly or indirectly involved in
the manufacturing of nearly every product, and is currently
undergoing a shift as its business processes and models become
digitized. Concurrently, calls for increased circular economy
practices and more sustainable operations are emerging from
an array of industries, offering the chemical industry major new
opportunities for growth. On the other hand, each individual
organization will need to be prepared to take a proactive—
rather than reactive—approach to technology innovation and
implementation, using available data to weigh opportunities and
risks; make effective, informed decisions; and gain a competitive
advantage. Data-informed models can help businesses benefit
from advances in technology, including through improved supply
chain management, preventative maintenance, and other costreduction strategies.


With the ability to measure and track technological progress,
public policymakers can analyze and promote technology
innovations in terms of societal goals. This ability to understand
and influence the drivers behind technology advancements
helps to remove the guesswork from the process of choosing and
developing the right technologies, offering governments insights
into how money and time can be invested to best meet their
objectives.

**Participant Profile**


Although the expertise found throughout this course can be applied to a wide series of
industries, it is especially beneficial for those working in sectors such as chemicals, life
sciences, manufacturing, investment, energy, and public policy. Though not limited to any
job level, the most common roles that will benefit from the course include:


**Research and development managers** looking to advance their organization’s technology
portfolio decision-making through data-informed insights.


**Production or manufacturing operations managers** who wish to incorporate data collection
and analysis to anticipate technological changes and prioritize their investments in innovation


**C-suite and executive level management** in technology-related firms in charge of
establishing a data-informed technology plan for their organization.


**Public policy makers** from technology-related sectors who want to discover how
technological progress can be measured and predicted to help meet societal goals.


**Private investors** interested in optimizing technology-related portfolios based on technology
performance data, allowing them to improve technology investment decisions.
