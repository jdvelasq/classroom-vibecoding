## About the Program

Sustainability is becoming more and more

prominent on corporate agendas. Once

considered a matter of compliance, it is currently

viewed as a key for increasing efficiency and a

source of competitive advantage. 90% of

executives acknowledge its importance but only

60% of companies have a sustainability strategy.

Hence, there is strong need for Chief

Sustainability Officers that interpret changes in

the external sustainability environment, work on

the strategic consequences, and define a

corporate strategy to tackle them properly.


The Chief Sustainability Officer Program from

MIT Professional Education provides you with the

## Learning Journey

Program Outline



Module 1

8 weeks



Leadership & Innovation



Module 2

8 weeks



Sustainability: Strategies and

Opportunities for Industry



With this module, participants will learn to lead by

coming to know themselves better, harnessing their

own creativity, and building teams and organizations

through the culture of innovation.



The time to act is upon us, and increased focus on
sustainability provides businesses with significant
benefits, from reductions in waste and costs to

additional opportunities for investment as consumer

behavior evolves.



Module 3

8 weeks



Life Cycle Assessment



Module 4

8 weeks



Sustainable Infrastructure

Systems: Planning and Operations



This module will provide a foundation for conducting
and interpreting LCAs, following the approach defined

in international standards. The steps include goal and
scope definition, life cycle inventory, life cycle impact

assessment, and interpretation.



You will explore systems theory as the basis for

analysis, development, and engineering of

sustainable, adaptable technological systems. This

module will equip you with the knowledge and skills

needed to develop modern sustainable infrastructure,

from public policy and planning to operational

management and data collection.



Module 5

9 weeks



Circular Economy



This module provides a fundamental understanding of

the meaning and evolution of a circular economy with

the role of material science, economics, and

institutional methods. Explore innovation and evolution

with a focus on circular economies at the corporate
level, through specific examples like water, metals and

waste recycling, and others.
