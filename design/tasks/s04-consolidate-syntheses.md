# S04 — Consolidate Independent Benchmark Syntheses

## Objective

Produce a single, evidence-aware consolidation of the professional-learning
syntheses created in S01 and S02 and the three independent benchmark syntheses
created in S03.

This task determines where the independent analyses converge, where they
diverge, what each contributes uniquely, and which findings are sufficiently
supported to constrain subsequent curriculum design.

This task is **synthesis integration**, not final curriculum design.

---

## Governing instructions

Read and comply with the repository-level `AGENTS.md` before performing this
task.

Analytics remains the organizing curricular identity. Machine Learning,
Statistics, Operations Research and Optimization, Data Science, Data
Engineering, Databases, Business Intelligence, Artificial Intelligence, and
related fields are contributing disciplines. Their inclusion in the
consolidation must be described according to their function within Analytics;
they must not redefine the target curriculum.

---

Read all five upstream synthesis outputs:

- `design/synthesis/s01-datacamp.md`
- `design/synthesis/s02-pluralsight.md`

- `design/synthesis/s03-synthesis-chatgpt.md`

If any required input is missing, unreadable, or clearly incomplete, stop and

## Source policy

The five S01--S03 synthesis files are the direct evidence base for this task.

Do not perform external web research. Do not rerun S01, S02, or S03. Do not
modify benchmark files, upstream synthesis files, or their source materials.

When a finding requires confirmation beyond what the syntheses make
traceable, preserve the uncertainty rather than resolving it through new
research.

---

## Integration principles

### Preserve independence

The purpose of S04 is to compare the independent S03 work after it was
completed and integrate the source-specific professional-learning syntheses,
not to erase differences between agents or providers.

Do not treat a finding as strong merely because several syntheses repeat it.
They analyzed overlapping evidence and may share the same underlying source.

Assess convergence in light of:

- agreement among the independent S03 syntheses;
- the specific provider evidence retained by S01 and S02;
- the benchmark evidence each synthesis cites;
- independence and diversity of the underlying evidence families;
- explicitness and depth of support;
- relevance to Analytics.

### Do not average away disagreement

When syntheses differ in scope, interpretation, prioritization, or confidence:

- state the disagreement clearly;
- identify the underlying evidence or interpretive choice when traceable;
- explain its implication for later design;
- retain the question where the available evidence cannot resolve it.

### Preserve the level of abstraction

Do not create a course title, syllabus, module sequence, weekly schedule,
final learning outcomes, final technology stack, or final assessment design.

S04 may identify constraints, tensions, and questions that later design must
address, but it must not make those later design decisions.

---

## Required analysis

Analyze the S01--S03 syntheses comparatively to determine:

1. their shared characterization of Analytics as an educational domain;
2. the role of each contributing discipline within Analytics;
3. recurring capabilities and knowledge areas;
4. evidence-supported progression patterns;
5. the role of authentic practice, data, tools, and technologies;
6. convergences among authoritative, institutional, literature-derived, and
   professional evidence as represented in the syntheses;
7. material disagreements, omissions, and uncertainty;
8. the implications that are sufficiently supported to constrain the next
   curriculum-design stage.

Distinguish among:

- **cross-synthesis convergence**: independently observed agreement;
- **source-supported convergence**: agreement that is traceable to diverse
  underlying benchmark evidence;
- **single-synthesis insight**: a useful observation not corroborated by the
  other syntheses;
- **disagreement or unresolved question**: a difference that must remain open.

## Required output

Create exactly one file:

`design/synthesis/s04-synthesis.md`

Use this structure:

### `# Consolidated Benchmark Synthesis`

### `## 1. Scope and integration method`

Name the five inputs, describe the comparative method, and state important
limitations.

### `## 2. Convergent account of Analytics`

State the strongest supported characterization of Analytics, while preserving
meaningful variation.

### `## 3. Analytics and contributing disciplines`

Consolidate the functions of adjacent disciplines without allowing any of them
to become the curricular identity.

### `## 4. Recurring capabilities and knowledge architecture`

Present the consolidated capabilities and knowledge areas, with their strength
of support and important variations.

### `## 5. Learning progression and authentic practice`

Consolidate supported progression patterns and the pedagogical role of cases,
projects, data, communication, decisions, and integrated work.

### `## 6. Tools, technologies, and implementation context`

Distinguish durable capabilities from enabling, provider-specific, emerging,
or specialized technologies.

### `## 7. Convergences, differences, and unique contributions`

Compare the three S03 syntheses explicitly and integrate the distinct
professional-learning evidence from S01 and S02. Separate strong convergence,
source-dependent convergence, useful single-synthesis insights, provider-
specific observations, and genuine disagreement.

### `## 8. Uncertainties and evidence limitations`

Document unresolved issues, gaps, source concentrations, and limitations that
must constrain interpretation.

### `## 9. Constraints for the next design stage`

State the evidence-backed constraints, choices, and questions that subsequent
curriculum design must address. Do not design the curriculum.

### `## 10. Traceability map`

Provide a compact mapping from major consolidated findings to the relevant
S01--S03 synthesis files and, where available in those files, their underlying
benchmark or provider evidence.
