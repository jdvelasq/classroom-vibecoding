# S02 — Pluralsight Professional Benchmark

## Objective

Build a structured professional benchmark of **Analytics and Data Science as represented by Pluralsight**.

The purpose of this task is to reconstruct, from current first-party evidence, how Pluralsight defines, structures, and operationalizes professional learning for Analytics- and Data Science-related roles.

This task is **benchmark construction**, not curriculum design.

The resulting document will become part of the evidence base used in later benchmark synthesis tasks.

---

## Governing instructions

Read and comply with the repository-level `AGENTS.md` before performing this task.

Do not impose a predefined project definition of Analytics or Data Science on Pluralsight.

For this benchmark, reconstruct Pluralsight's own representation of these fields from its published learning structures and descriptions.

---

## Required output

Create:

`design/synthesis/s02-pluralsight.md`

This is the only required output of this task.

Do not modify the benchmark corpus or synthesis outputs belonging to other
tasks.

## Source policy

Use current **first-party Pluralsight sources** as the primary evidence.

Relevant sources may include:

- learning paths;
- role-oriented paths;
- skill assessments;
- individual courses;
- projects or labs;
- competency descriptions;
- professional role descriptions;
- certification-related learning structures;
- prerequisite or sequencing information;
- official Pluralsight documentation describing learning structures.

Prefer Pluralsight's own pages over search-result summaries, blogs about Pluralsight, reviews, or third-party descriptions.

Secondary sources may be used only when necessary to locate or clarify first-party material. Do not use them as substitutes for available first-party evidence.

Do not rely on prior model knowledge when the corresponding information can be verified from current Pluralsight sources.

Record the access date for the benchmark.

## Scope

The benchmark must focus on Pluralsight's explicit professional-learning structures related to **Analytics** and **Data Science**.

### Analytics

Identify Pluralsight learning structures explicitly associated with roles or areas such as:

- Data Analyst;
- Business Analyst;
- Analytics;
- Business Analytics;

and other closely related roles or paths that Pluralsight itself places within this professional space.

Do not assume that all of these labels currently exist as formal Pluralsight paths. Report only structures that can be verified.

### Data Science

Identify Pluralsight learning structures explicitly associated with roles or areas such as:

- Data Scientist;
- Data Science;

and closely related variants defined by Pluralsight.

Again, report only structures that can be verified.

### Adjacent fields

Inspect adjacent Pluralsight learning structures only when they help establish the composition or boundaries of Analytics or Data Science.

Potentially relevant adjacent areas include:

- Machine Learning;
- Statistics;
- Data Engineering;
- Databases;
- SQL;
- Business Intelligence;
- Artificial Intelligence;
- programming.

Do not turn this task into a general inventory of Pluralsight.

## Research questions

The benchmark must provide sufficient evidence to address the following questions.

### 1. Professional roles

How does Pluralsight characterize Analytics-related professional roles?

How does Pluralsight characterize Data Science-related professional roles?

For each relevant role, identify where evidence permits:

- expected activities;
- competencies;
- methods;
- tools;
- professional outputs;
- relationship with other roles.

Preserve Pluralsight's terminology.

---

### 2. Learning structures

What learning paths, role-oriented paths, skill structures, or equivalent structures does Pluralsight provide for these roles?

For each relevant structure, record where available:

- official name;
- stated purpose;
- intended learner or professional role;
- constituent courses or major components;
- stated duration;
- ordering;
- prerequisites;
- projects, labs, or assessments.

Do not assume that the ordering of items necessarily represents prerequisite dependency unless Pluralsight indicates this.

---

### 3. Curriculum composition

What knowledge and skill areas constitute Pluralsight's Analytics learning structures?

What knowledge and skill areas constitute its Data Science learning structures?

Reconstruct these areas from the evidence.

Do **not** begin with a fixed taxonomy and force Pluralsight's content into it.

Higher-level categories may be developed during analysis when they help summarize the evidence, but they must remain traceable to the underlying Pluralsight structures.

---

### 4. Learning progression

How does learning progress within the relevant structures?

Identify, where evidence permits:

- foundations;
- intermediate capabilities;
- advanced or specialized capabilities;
- prerequisite relationships;
- transitions between skill areas;
- progression from concepts to application.

Clearly distinguish:

- progression explicitly stated by Pluralsight;
- progression implied by path structure;
- progression inferred analytically.

---

### 5. Relative emphasis

What receives substantial instructional attention within the relevant Pluralsight structures?

Use multiple observable indicators where available, including:

- instructional time;
- number of courses;
- recurrence across paths;
- position within learning sequences;
- prerequisite relationships;
- projects or labs;
- assessments;
- repeated use of particular methods or tools.

Do not treat course count alone as a measure of curricular importance.

When exact instructional time cannot be established, do not fabricate estimates.

---

### 6. Analytics versus Data Science

Explicitly compare Pluralsight's Analytics-related and Data Science-related learning structures.

Identify:

- shared foundations;
- shared tools;
- shared methods;
- competencies emphasized in both;
- competencies emphasized primarily in Analytics;
- competencies emphasized primarily in Data Science;
- differences in methodological depth;
- differences in programming expectations;
- differences in professional activities;
- differences in expected outputs;
- differences in learning progression.

The purpose is not to determine which field is broader, more advanced, or more important.

The purpose is to document how Pluralsight **operationally differentiates** them.

---

### 7. Contributing disciplines

Identify important contributions from fields such as:

- Machine Learning;
- Statistics;
- Operations Research and Optimization;
- Data Engineering;
- Databases;
- Business Intelligence;
- Artificial Intelligence;
- software/programming disciplines.

For each substantial contribution, determine its apparent function within the relevant Pluralsight learning structure.

Follow the curriculum-identity rules established in `AGENTS.md`.

The presence of material from a contributing discipline must not automatically be interpreted as evidence that the discipline defines the overall learning structure.

---

### 8. Practice orientation

Determine how Pluralsight connects learning with professional practice.

Look for evidence including:

- exercises;
- projects;
- labs;
- realistic datasets;
- case-based work;
- workflows;
- tool use;
- assessments;
- certifications.

Distinguish between claims made by Pluralsight and conclusions inferred from the structure of the learning materials.

---

### 9. Tools and technologies

Document the principal tools and technologies appearing across the relevant Analytics and Data Science structures.

Where the evidence supports it, distinguish technologies that appear to be:

- foundational;
- frequently used;
- role-specific;
- specialized;
- optional.

The purpose is descriptive.

Do not create a technology wishlist or recommend technologies for the project's eventual curriculum.

## Evidence requirements

Every substantive claim about Pluralsight's current offerings must be traceable to inspected evidence.

For important sources, preserve at least:

- resource or page title;
- URL;
- type of resource;
- relevant role, path, or course;
- information extracted from the source.

The final benchmark must make it possible to revisit the original evidence.

Prefer stable canonical URLs when available.

Avoid unnecessary duplication of sources.

### Evidence versus inference

Maintain a clear distinction among:

1. **Provider-stated evidence** — Pluralsight explicitly states something.
2. **Structural evidence** — something is observable from the organization of Pluralsight's paths, courses, labs, or other materials.
3. **Analytical inference** — a conclusion is drawn from multiple pieces of evidence.

Never present an analytical inference as a direct Pluralsight claim.

If important information cannot be verified, state the limitation explicitly.

Do not fill evidence gaps using model knowledge.

---

## Analysis rules

### Preserve provider terminology

Use Pluralsight's terminology when describing Pluralsight's own structures.

Do not silently rename roles, paths, competencies, or categories to fit terminology preferred by this project.

### Reconstruct before comparing

First understand Analytics and Data Science independently as represented by Pluralsight.

Only then compare them.

Do not begin from an assumed distinction between the two.

### Avoid exhaustive cataloguing

The goal is to reconstruct the architecture of the relevant professional-learning structures, not to reproduce Pluralsight's complete catalog.

Include enough course-level detail to support the analysis.

### Avoid premature curriculum design

Do not recommend:

- the final course structure;
- weeks or modules;
- learning outcomes for the project's course;
- assignments;
- technologies to adopt;
- topics to add;
- topics to remove;
- instructional hours for the eventual course.

Those decisions belong to later synthesis and curriculum-design tasks.

### Avoid disciplinary substitution

Do not reinterpret Analytics simply as:

- introductory Data Science;
- applied Machine Learning;
- applied Statistics;
- Business Intelligence;
- Data Engineering;
- Operations Research;
- a collection of software tools.

Follow `AGENTS.md` when interpreting relationships among these fields.
