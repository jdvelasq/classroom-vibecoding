### **Overview**

Rapid prototyping serves various purposes, with cost-effectiveness and a decreased rate of product failure

being the primary advantages. As the world emerges from recent economic disruptions, the evolving scope

of product development presents unique opportunities for product professionals and business leaders

alike, with rapid prototyping emerging at the top.


The MIT xPRO **Rapid Prototyping Methodologies for Commercial Application** program imparts an intimate

understanding of product performance, fewer design iterations, and enhanced early understanding of

scaling and manufacturability issues. MIT developed the plastic face shield, an integral part of PPE suits

during COVID-19, and generated three million face shields in less than 30 days for 4,000 frontline facilities

fighting the virus.


Faculty member Martin Culpepper, professor of mechanical engineering and director of Project Manus,

MIT’s research and development office dedicated to helping create and expand access to technology, led

the mass production process with his expertise in rapid prototyping and 3D cutting and printing. Culpepper

described their technique as one that “combines low-cost materials with high-rate manufacturing,”

a description that embodies rapid prototyping as a whole.


This program offers product engineers, managers, designers, business leaders, and functional heads a

cutting-edge perspective on the use of rapid prototyping technology, paired with the knowledge needed to

streamline product design and manufacturing. Participants will come away with a thorough understanding

of different types of fabrication processes and the ability to discern the best strategy for building and

evaluating a prototype and ultimately giving their organization an advantage in the go-to-market process.

### **Key Takeaways**

Identify rapid prototyping fundamentals and the various techniques for developing the prototypes.

### **Program Curriculum**

Module 1
##### **The Rapid Prototyping Landscape**


Discuss what rapid prototyping means to you.

You will:


Define the goals that need to be achieved through a rapid prototype

Understand the uses of the different tools and technologies used for rapid prototyping

Compare common fabrication methods used in rapid prototyping

Identify the benefits and fundamental issues of different fabrication processes used in rapid prototyping

Quantify the benefits of rapid prototypes and different fabrication processes in an engineering design process


Module 2
##### **Concept Prototype Attribute Mapping**


Understand the benefits of developing a concept prototype (CP).

You will:


Compare the characteristics of a concept prototype (CP) and the desired product (DP)

Analyze the metrics for selecting appropriate fabrication processes

Estimate the common costs of practical import

Review the design attributes of user and prototype requirements

Calculate the mass, mass moment of inertia, prototype cost, and product cost to develop a rapid prototype

Measure the fabrication attributes and DFM consistency

Identify the design errors in a DFF and DFM sample


Module 3
##### **Common “Go-To” Rapid Prototyping Technologies**


Identify the characteristics of manufacturing processes — serial and parallel fabrication processes.

You will compare different serial fabrication processes to determine the most cost-effective, time-saving

process and list the pros and cons of:


3D printing fabrication

2D laser cutting fabrication

2D abrasive waterjet cutting fabrication

3D machining CNC milling fabrication

3D machining CNC turning fabrication


Module 4
##### **Traditional Fabrication Processes**


Compare the different parallel fabrication processes, such as the serial and parallel fabrication processes, to

determine the most cost-effective and time-saving process in a given scenario.

You will also list the pros and cons of:



Silicone molding fabrication

Low-temp metal fabrication

Thermoforming fabrication

Sheet metal fabrication

Module 5
##### **Smart Design, Fabrication, and Performance Assessment Decisions**


Illustrate the general framework for creating a basic prototyping plan.

You will:


Identify the possible constraints in developing a prototype

Establish nontechnical and technical goals and requirements for a prototype

Link the DFM and geometry of a concept prototype and the desired product

Compare the risks and benefits of a prototype

Devise fabrication decisions to define the strategies for creating a prototype and assessing the prototype


Module 6
##### **Designing Concept Prototypes (CP) That Emulate Desired Products (DP)**


Develop a hypothesis for a desired product.

You will:


Analyze the attributes of a concept prototype based on a hypothesis

Test a virtual concept prototype

Identify the pros and cons of a virtual concept prototype

Determine the characteristics of a virtual prototype and a physical prototype

Assess the data gathered from testing a concept prototype based on a hypothesis


Module 7
##### **Assessing the Cost and Value of Rapid Prototyping**


Identify how rapid prototyping can be leveraged to reduce cost and increase quality.

You will:


Calculate both the nontechnical and technical cost of a rapid prototype

Evaluate the overall cost of developing a prototype

Recognize how to optimize the cost value of a rapid prototype

Discuss the cost value of using different processes for developing a prototype

Assess how to optimize the cost value for a rapid prototype


Module 8
##### **The Future of Manufacturing**


Summarize the future of machine learning in designing and modeling rapid prototypes.

You will:


Recognize the role of rapid prototyping in medical research and development into practice

Discuss how rapid prototyping can be leveraged to enable advances for human health and welfare in

developing countries

Identify how 3D printing is used in the rapid prototype manufacturing process

### **Activities and Assignments** **Capstone Project**

Modules 5–8 will feature a capstone assignment divided into four parts across the four modules.


Participants will receive two options of prototypes to work on: the face shield and the beam steering

gyroscope for a satellite.


Part A: Participants will make fabrication decisions for a prototype.


Part B: Participants will assess the data results for the different processes used.


Part C: Participants will conduct a cost analysis to determine the most efficient process to use to

build the prototype.


Part D: Participants will create a presentation/proposal based on the information gathered

in Parts A–C.


Each assignment will be Level 3 graded and will have custom feedback.

Professor Culpepper’s research concentrates on the

invention, design, and fabrication of high-performance


machine systems. He focuses on inventing new

technologies in areas where conventional technology

a great engineer means learning to meld invention,


engineering common sense, and engineering science

with practical fabrication/measurement skills to

create parts/products/systems that solve


real problems.
