Key Takeaways


This course employs MIT’s signature hands-on approach to Cloud Computing and

DevOps with a foundation steeped in theory. You will gain technical skills with a two
pronged approach including theoretical and application-oriented perspectives. Study the

mechanisms and tools to take advantage of Cloud Computing and DevOps as secure and
affordable software design and efficient technological operations.


After completing this course, you will:



01 Become familiar with the past,

present, and future of Cloud

Computing and its impacts on

new developments in

technology and business.


02 Gain immediately applicable

experience with the key tools for
effective software design, Cloud

Computing, and DevOps

methodologies.


03 Understand the technicalities of

containers, orchestration,

dockers, and KPIs and how

these tools are best applied in

today’s work environment.



04 Optimize both security and

performance in software design with a

robust and scalable cloud

infrastructure.


05 Distinguish the advantages and
disadvantages of different applications,

such as Serverless and Cloud Native,

and how to leverage them.


06 Identify and implement the appropriate

approaches to develop and streamline

Cloud Computing and DevOps
projects.

able to explain how the HTTP, HTML, and URL specifications, created by Sir Tim

Berners-Lee, helped solve these problems. Finally, you will be able to describe the
different generations of the web and the importance of smartphones as a means of
collecting data.

Module 6
Serverless

Serverless computing is a cloud execution model in which a provider activates the server

and dynamically manages the allocation of resources to machines. According to AWS,

serverless is the cloud-native architecture that allows us to delegate our operational

responsibilities to the cloud provider. In turn, this makes it possible to increase our agility,

speed of implementation, and ability to innovate. Examples of serverless applications

include warehouses, chatbots, natural language processing, image recognition, databases,
;API gateways, messaging services, and expressions (Lambda).
